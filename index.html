      About this mockup

      The site shows how the platform would look and behave. The tract, its stands, the point clouds and every value are generated in your browser for illustration.

      Data and methods

      Base maps

      Every map on the site can sit on an online base map or on a plain map that works offline. People can pick the base map, make the stand colors more or less see-through, turn stand numbers, roads and streams and the locator map on or off, or paste the address of their own tile service, such as a county or agency imagery server. Their choices are kept in their own browser.

      Reference data

      The tract outline follows National Forest System roads 513 and 521 and an unnamed tributary of Lee Creek, on national forest land near Ratcliff, Texas. Roads come from the USDA Forest Service road layer and the USGS National Transportation Dataset, streams and lakes from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset, and county lines from the US Census Bureau. Plantation blocks were traced from USDA NAIP aerial imagery. Stand histories and every forest value are invented.

      Layers in the mockup

      How the example values are made

      Each stand is given a planting year, a site index, a planting density and a management history of thinnings and prescribed burns. A simple growth model then carries basal area, height and stem count forward one year at a time from planting to 2026. Fuel builds up with the years since the last burn, and water stress follows a dry or wet index for each year.

      The three risk indices combine those values on a scale of 0 to 100. Wildfire risk rises with fuel load, understory and dry weather. Drought risk rises with stand density and sandy soil. Pest risk rises with density, water stress and bark beetle activity in the stand or next to it. The priority rules read the same values: an active beetle spot is urgent, a dense stand needs thinning, and heavy fuel calls for a burn or, where trees are too young to burn under, fuel reduction.

      The 3D point clouds are drawn from the same stand record, so tree height, spacing, thinned rows, understory and dead crowns match the numbers on the maps.
